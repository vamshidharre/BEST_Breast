01 · The vessel

Meet the DD-Boat

DD-Boat 11 on the Guerlédan dock, before a lake run.

~1 HzGPS position fixes
~9 Hzcontrol loop and logging
Py 3.5on board, NumPy only

Two thrusters, no rudder

It steers by running the left and right motors at different speeds.

Raspberry Pi 3 + Arduino Mega

The Pi runs our Python code on board; the Arduino drives the motors.

9-axis IMU as a compass

The LIS3MDL magnetometer gives the heading after calibration.

GPS receiver

About one position fix per second, converted to metres on a local map.

Wi-Fi + SSH from a laptop

We start missions remotely and pull the logs back over the lake Wi-Fi.

03 · Control

How the boat steers itself

Where is the waypoint?
θ* = atan2(xt − x, yt − y)
How far off is our heading? (kept within ±180°)
e = sawtooth(θ* − θ)
Set the two motors (clipped to 0…115)
uL = 100 + 35·e    uR = 100 − 35·e
Way off course? Spin on the spot

If |e| > 40°, motors go to ±95 until |e| < 20°. A waypoint counts as reached within 3.5 m, or once we cross the line through it.

Control law from Prof. Luc Jaulin's DD-Boat course
04 · Sensors

Making the compass tell the truth

Field problem: East and West were swapped

After moving our code to a new boat, North and South read correctly, but pointing East read about 270° and pointing West about 90°.

Fix: flip the Y axis in the heading

θ = atan2(−my, mx)
applied in calib.py, waypoint_mission.py and regatta_loop.py.

05 · Positioning

From GPS degrees to metres on the lake

Near the lake the Earth is almost flat, so we turn latitude and longitude into a simple x/y grid in metres around a fixed origin.

x = R · cos(lat₀) · (lon − lon₀)  East
y = R · (lat − lat₀)  North

R = 6 371 km · origin 48.199111° N, 3.014930° W (Guerlédan reference)

09 · Telemetry

What the sprint log tells us

A → B was slow: 0.59 m/s

281 s for 166 m. The heading error kept passing 40°, so the boat made 72 spin turns (81 s spinning).

B → A was 50% faster: 0.89 m/s

Only 10 spin turns. Both motors stayed close to full power.

Simulation vs lake

The twin predicted about 140 s for A → B; the real leg took twice as long. The box took 111 s vs 84 s simulated.

10 · Reliability

Built so we never lose the boat or the data

Run inside screen

Learned on the sprint: started from SSH, it stopped when Wi-Fi dropped. screen -S sprint keeps it running on the Pi.

GPS watchdog

No GPS fix for 3 seconds: both motors go to zero. No blind driving.

Track autosave

The KML track is written to disk every 5 seconds, so a power cut loses almost nothing.

Emergency stop

stop.py sets both motors to 0 at once.

Auto download

A laptop script waits for the boat to reconnect, then pulls the KML and CSV logs.

Everything logged

Position, target, heading, error and both motor commands, about 9 times per second.
